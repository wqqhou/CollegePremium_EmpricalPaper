NET-WORTH SAMPLE REQUIREMENT: SENSITIVITY RESULTS

Change: retain respondents with missing 1997 household net worth. Income,
ASVAB, demographic controls, 2015 degree status, and usable earnings in all
three years remain required. Missing wealth is not imputed or used in regression.

Official custom weights were downloaded for the expanded 3,171-person sample.
The weighted bottom-income quintile is recalculated using this sample and weights.
The 1996 nominal-dollar cutoff changes from $21,360 to $21,613.
Among the original 2,849 respondents, 16 change income-group classification.
The comparison therefore includes the sample expansion, corresponding reweighting,
and updated sample-relative income cutoff.

All earnings estimates are annual employee earnings in 2025 US dollars.
BA means highest degree is bachelor's only by 2015; high-school diploma is the
reference within each income group. The difference is lower income minus above
cutoff. Weighted pooled OLS uses the same controls and 2016/2018/2020 earnings
as the current paper, with respondent-clustered CR1 standard errors.

Interpretation: the estimated BA premium gap is almost unchanged; dropping the
net-worth completeness rule does not materially change the main finding in this
comparison. This does not eliminate other complete-case or degree-selection issues.
The joint test now rejects all four interactions being zero at the 5% level.
The three non-BA interaction coefficients remain individually insignificant at 5%.
